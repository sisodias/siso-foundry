## P1 — prove the Agency OS path

7. **Module dossiers** — directly review each Agency candidate cluster for source revision, license, maintenance, security, tenancy, data ownership, API coverage, deployment burden, integration mode, alternative cost, and client job.
8. **Capability adapter proof** — integrate one representative module through SISO identity, capability, approval, audit, metering, and event contracts; compare this with direct embedding.
9. **Control-plane ownership** — register or identify the owning SISO Agency Work before promoting the six recurring business-glue gaps out of Foundry.
10. **Offer catalog and stack recipes** — turn reviewed capabilities into General Agency and industry offers with deduplicated dependencies, delivery costs, prices, managed services, and explicit client outcomes.
11. **Outcome loop** — record cost saved, time saved, quality, risk, willingness to pay, renewal, and agent autonomy achieved by every deployed capability, then feed the result back into adoption and economic evidence.

## P2 — scale across industries

12. **Industry maps** — turn recurring business jobs, regulations, data objects, source modules, offer economics, and outcome receipts into Library-visible industry capability maps.
13. **Portfolio sweeps** — evaluate high-signal repositories by risk-adjusted three-year value per engineering month, capability gap, and business outcome—not stars alone.
14. **Owner promotion** — move verified patterns and packages to their owning Works; Foundry keeps evidence and lineage, not duplicate product source.
15. **Retirement loop** — explicitly retire stale candidates, market anchors, prices, and superseded verdicts so agents do not keep re-deriving dead paths.

## Promotion rule

Discovery is not release. A candidate advances only through direct source review, rights review, behavior or integration proof, owner assignment, an immutable release, Library indexing, and—when it belongs in Agency OS—an explicit capability adapter with policy and audit evidence.
